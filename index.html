What this is

A prototype Market Value Analysis for El Paso County, built in the spirit of Reinvestment Fund's MVA — a data-based, field-validatable tool that clusters small geographies into market types to guide investment and revitalization strategy. Like the original, it works at a sub-neighborhood geography (Census block groups) and produces an internally-referenced index: types are only meaningful relative to each other within El Paso, not compared to other cities' MVAs.

Six indicators were standardized (z-scored) and clustered with k-means into six market types, A (highest value / least distress) through F (most distressed): median sale price per sq ft, median days-on-market, new residential permits per 1,000 units, owner-occupancy rate, vacancy rate, and code-enforcement violations per 1,000 units.

Sources
Home sales — hoffmanap/housetrends (27,448 geocoded closed sales since 2023, true block-group median $/sqft); days-on-market supplemented from hoffmanap/MLSSales tract-level stats
Parcels & permits — hoffmanap/permits, City of El Paso Planning & Inspections (42,603 permits, live ArcGIS feed)
Code enforcement — City of El Paso case list (Nov 2024), filtered to physical-blight categories, geocoded via Census Bureau batch geocoder
Foreclosures — El Paso County Clerk's live Foreclosure Map feed, snapshotted monthly (see below)
Demographics & housing stock — ACS 2020–2024 5-year estimates (via Census Reporter), block-group level
Geography — US Census TIGERweb, El Paso County block groups

Foreclosures: the county's searchable foreclosure form is CAPTCHA-gated and only returns legal descriptions, not addresses or coordinates. A companion map feed on the same county site is open and geocoded, but only ever shows the next posted sale date (Texas law requires posting 21+ days ahead) — so rather than one static pull, a scheduled job snapshots it monthly and accumulates a real history over time. Not yet enough months of data to use as a clustering input; this will fold in once a meaningful history builds up.

Known gaps vs. a full MVA: no subsidized-housing layer; foreclosure history still accumulating (see above).
